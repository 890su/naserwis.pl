How to check CCTV before you need the recordings

Live video is only one check. Test playback, the picture after dark and notifications too. Start with a short list you can repeat.

NaSerwis editorial team · Updated: 24.10.2026

Check recordings as well as live video

Open recordings from every camera: today, an earlier day and the oldest period you need to keep. Check dates and unexpected gaps. Export a short clip and play it outside the recorder.

  • For motion-triggered recording, make a controlled walk through the monitored area.
  • If recordings are missing, do not format the drive before finding the cause.

Recorder not recording? See what a diagnosis covers

Compare daytime and night images

Can you see what the camera was installed to show: an entrance, gate or walkway? Check for a shifted camera or plants blocking the view. After dark, look for infrared reflections and a bright haze.

  • Inspect the cover for dirt, cobwebs, cracks and moisture.
  • Clean only when needed, using the method allowed in your model’s instructions. Do not polish the cover or use solvents.

Inspect cables and power without opening equipment

Look at accessible cables and housings. Damaged insulation, loose mounts or water marks need attention. Do not open a powered recorder or power supply; leave work at height to someone with suitable training.

Analogue cameras need video-path and power checks. IP cameras also involve the network or PoE. A hybrid system can use both.

Test phone access and notifications separately

Open the cameras over mobile data and trigger one agreed event in the detection area. Confirm that it produces both a recording and a notification. Viewing over Wi-Fi at the premises does not test the whole remote path.

Viewing or detection fault? See the common symptoms

Record the result and repeat checks after changes

A short note is enough: date, camera number, check, result and next action. Repeat relevant checks after a router change, power cut, building work or severe weather. Choose the frequency to suit the conditions and manufacturer’s advice.

Start with an equipment and connection inventory

Record camera and NVR/DVR models, software versions, channel numbers and power arrangements. For IP, include topology, PoE ports and addressing. For analogue, note CVBS/AHD/HD-TVI/HD-CVI format, cable, BNC connectors and twisted-pair video transmitters where fitted.

For a hybrid system, check the specific DVR’s channel and resolution limits. Analogue inputs and an Ethernet port alone do not establish compatibility with every camera.

Confirm archive coverage and export

Check storage status, error messages, schedules, system time and recording channels. Compare the oldest available footage with your required retention period; check again after changing image quality.

Test playback and export. For events, check pre-event and post-event recording where supported. Preserve important clips before changes; drive initialisation is not a test that preserves recordings.

Separate IP, analogue and night-image checks

On the IP side, check port stability, connection errors and available PoE budget where used. On the analogue side, check connectors, power and signal compatibility with the input. Lines or dropouts alone do not identify one failed component.

Assess day and night images separately with the cover fitted. Dirt and infrared reflections may only become apparent after dark. Change one parameter at a time.

Verify events, permissions and changes

Use a controlled event to check zones, sensitivity, arming schedules and notification recipients. Confirm local recording and remote viewing separately.

Before an update, back up configuration where supported and check the package and upgrade path against the manufacturer’s instructions. Agree downtime and repeat tests afterwards. Remove unused installer accounts while preserving owner access.

Set a schedule for the installation

The table below is an example workflow, not a standard or a mandatory interval for every installation. Device instructions and site conditions take precedence. Arrange a separate technical inspection of mounts, seals and power.

The inspection record should identify the channel, playback result, oldest recording date, errors found, changes made and who is responsible for follow-up.

An example inspection schedule

A starting point to adapt. Harsh conditions or critical recordings may call for more frequent checks.

WhenWhat to check
WeeklyEvery camera’s picture, sample playback, dates and error messages.
MonthlyA trial export, night images, detection and notifications, accessible cables and covers. Clean only if needed.
After a change or incidentAfter a router change, update, power cut or work on the system: recheck video, recording and access.
As specified for the equipment and siteTechnical inspection of mounts, seals, power and installation condition. Set the interval for the particular models.

A short inspection record

Date · camera/channel · day and night picture · playback and export · oldest available recording · alarms/errors · action and next check. Keep passwords out of this log.
