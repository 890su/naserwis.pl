How to improve home Wi-Fi coverage and stability

If you can run Ethernet to your computer or access point, start with that. If you can’t, test the alternatives in your home before buying equipment.

Find where the problem starts

Try the same task beside the router and in the problem room. Repeat with another phone or computer. If it works nearby but not farther away, start with coverage and placement.

If internet is also slow over cable, another extender may not help. Check the router and provider connection. Do not reset settings without knowing how to restore them.

Use cable wherever practical

Connect a desktop computer by Ethernet. Do the same for access points and compatible Mesh units. They can then receive data over cable rather than through walls before sending it to phones.

Your phone still connects over Wi-Fi even when the access point uses a cable. A short cable from a computer to a Mesh unit won’t fix a weak wireless link between that unit and the router.

No cable route? Try Powerline

A Powerline kit sends data through electrical wiring. Connect one adapter to the router and the other to a computer or Wi-Fi point.

It may work better than weak Wi-Fi through difficult walls, but wiring and appliances can also cause slowdowns and delay spikes.

  • Plug directly into wall sockets as instructed, rather than filtered extension strips.
  • Test while household appliances are running.
  • Compare dropouts and call stability as well as download speed.

Place wireless Mesh or repeaters where they still receive a good signal

Do not put an extender in the dead zone behind the wall that caused the problem. Move it to a place where it receives a usable connection from its upstream point.

Keep the path to that point and to client devices as open as practical. Avoid metal cupboards, large mirrors and bulky appliances. You don’t need a clear line of sight between every pair of points. Check the connection each point actually uses.

Check whether the change helped

Repeat in the same rooms on the same device. Test a video call and try downloading at the same time. Frequent interruptions and delay spikes can matter more than one impressive speed result.

Move an existing point before adding more. Extra Mesh units do not automatically improve the network.

Separate radio, WAN and client limitations

Compare one client near the router, at its intended location and over Ethernet where possible. Repeat with another client. Poor wired performance does not establish a Wi-Fi coverage fault.

Record conditions and test the LAN and internet path separately. Latency to a public host includes the provider and external route.

Prefer Ethernet for stationary clients and backhaul

Backhaul carries traffic from an access point to the rest of the network. Ethernet to an AP or compatible Mesh unit removes wall attenuation and shared airtime from this segment. Wireless clients still use the access point’s local radio.

Check supported backhaul, port rates and router/AP topology in the exact model documentation. Avoid accidental loops. Ethernet from a PC to a satellite does not make the satellite’s wireless backhaul wired.

Qualify Powerline on the actual electrical installation

PLC uses a separate medium, not twisted-pair Ethernet. Circuits, phases, joints and electrical noise affect throughput, loss and jitter. AV/G.hn headline rates are not application throughput.

If a new Ethernet route is unavailable, compare PLC against the viable wireless backhaul. Test different sockets, traffic loads and appliances. Better latency and stability are possible, not guaranteed.

Design the real radio path

A wireless node needs a good uplink. Minimise obstacles on that path and between the node and its clients. An open path helps, but strict optical line of sight is not a Wi-Fi requirement.

Frequency, thickness, metal layers, moisture and angle affect attenuation. Concrete floors, mirrors and metal partitions can justify a different position or wired uplink. Nodes do not require full mutual visibility.

Measure loss and delay under load

Compare RTT to the router and an external host, packet loss and variability, idle and during transfers. ICMP can be deprioritised, so compare it with actual application behaviour.

A local test to a wired device helps isolate LAN performance from WAN. A 100 Mb/s or 1G port can also be a bottleneck. Change one variable at a time and preserve the baseline.

What weakens a Wi-Fi signal?

Indicative scale: 1 — small impact, 5 — very large. A placement guide, not a measurement or a percentage of lost speed. Thickness, moisture, metal layers, frequency and the angle through a surface change the result.

ObstacleImpactWhat matters
Furniture board / chipboard1–2Several layers and coatings can increase loss.
