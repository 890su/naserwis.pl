# Keyword Planner research — Warsaw

Checked in the authenticated Google Ads Keyword Planner on 2026-08-23. Network:
Google. Geography: Warsaw city. Historical period shown by Google: August 2025
through July 2026.

## Findings

- Polish service-specific terms such as `konfiguracja routera warszawa`,
  `montaż sieci lan warszawa`, `okablowanie strukturalne warszawa`,
  `serwis sieci komputerowych warszawa` and `serwis it warszawa` each showed
  the 10–100 average monthly search band.
- `serwis it warszawa` showed medium competition and a top-of-page bid range of
  approximately 3.82–19.48 PLN. Many narrower network terms had insufficient
  bid data; `montaż sieci lan warszawa` showed high competition.
- Google showed 100–1,000 monthly searches for `naprawa laptopów warszawa` and
  `naprawa komputerów warszawa`; the main page explicitly offers laptop and
  computer repair. These terms were moved into a separate ad group so they do
  not consume the network-services budget invisibly.
- Russian, Ukrainian and English Warsaw-specific seed terms each showed the
  0–10 monthly band. These language campaigns should therefore remain separate
  for reporting and message control, but launch only in the core zone with
  modest budgets to avoid unnecessary fragmentation.
- Keyword Planner displayed a warning that additional statistics require a live
  campaign. No campaign was created or enabled during this research.

## Implications for launch

1. PL receives the largest initial share, but its exact budget remains TBD.
2. RU, UK and EN use phrase plus exact match and a shared operating rule—not a
   shared Google Ads budget—so each language can be paused independently.
3. `B-WARSAW-FAR` can start paused if the approved daily budget is small;
   `C-OUTSIDE` always starts paused.
4. Add negatives for phone repair and unconfirmed component-only services,
   employment, courses and DIY intent before enabling traffic.
5. Re-evaluate search terms and language/geographic economics after enough
   qualified or paid jobs, not based on click-through rate alone.
